## Project Overview

**From Browsing to Buying: Product Engagement, Visitor Type, and Online Purchase Conversion**

This project investigates browsing behavior and purchase conversion among new and returning online shoppers through data preparation, exploratory data analysis, logistic regression, interaction analysis, and model diagnostics.

**Research question:** How does the relationship between engagement with product-related pages and the likelihood of completing an online purchase differ between new and returning visitors, after accounting for other browsing behaviors and session characteristics?

# A. Data Preparation & Exploratory Data Analysis

**Person 1**

## Data Preparation Decisions

- No imputation was necessary because no missing values were identified.
- Excluded 85 sessions classified as `Other`, restricting the analysis to new and returning visitors.
- Retained identical records provisionally because no unique session identifiers are available. The original data contained 125 duplicate rows beyond their first occurrences.
- Did not remove observations solely because of zero browsing durations or potential IQR outliers.
- Recoded `Revenue` as 0 (no purchase) and 1 (purchase); converted categorical predictors to factors with `New_Visitor` as the visitor-type reference level.
- Added `Duration_Minutes` and `Log_Duration = log1p(Duration_Minutes)` while preserving the original duration variable.

## EDA Results

| Metric | New visitors | Returning visitors |
|---|---:|---:|
| Sessions | 1,694 | 10,551 |
| Purchasing sessions | 422 | 1,470 |
| Conversion rate | 24.91% | 13.93% |
| Mean product browsing duration (minutes) | 10.61 | 21.49 |

- Overall conversion rate: 15.45% (1,892 purchasing sessions).
- Conversion generally increased with product browsing duration in both visitor groups. The longest duration group included only 15 new-visitor sessions, so its conversion rate should be interpreted cautiously.
- In the original sample, log transformation reduced duration skewness from 7.262 to -0.076.
- Bounce rate and exit rate (r = 0.913) and product page count and duration (r = 0.861) showed strong correlations in the analytical sample.

These are unadjusted descriptive findings, not causal conclusions or evidence of a statistically significant interaction.

# B. Statistical Modeling & Inference

**Person 2**

## Modeling Approach

Multiple logistic regression was used to examine whether the association between product browsing duration and purchase likelihood differs between new and returning visitors, while controlling for product page count and weekend status.

The modeling section includes:

1. Model formulation
2. Covariate selection
3. Multiple logistic regression
4. Interaction model
5. Model comparison
6. Statistical inference and interpretation
7. Predicted purchase probabilities

## Model Specification

| Variable | Role |
|---|---|
| `Revenue` | Binary outcome |
| `Duration_Minutes` | Primary engagement predictor |
| `VisitorType` | Moderator |
| `ProductRelated` | Control variable |
| `Weekend` | Control variable |

Two nested logistic regression models were fitted using the cleaned analytical dataset.

### Model 1: Main Effects Model

```r
model1 <- glm(
  Revenue ~ Duration_Minutes + VisitorType +
    ProductRelated + Weekend,
  data = df_clean,
  family = binomial(link = "logit")
)
```

### Model 2: Interaction Model

```r
model2 <- glm(
  Revenue ~ Duration_Minutes * VisitorType +
    ProductRelated + Weekend,
  data = df_clean,
  family = binomial(link = "logit")
)
```

## Modeling Decisions

- Used the cleaned analytical dataset generated in Section A without additional exclusions.
- Retained the original covariates specified in the project proposal.
- Used browsing duration in minutes to support intuitive coefficient interpretation.
- Retained `Log_Duration` as an alternative predictor to evaluate during model diagnostics.
- Included an interaction between browsing duration and visitor type to directly address the research question.
- Used new visitors and weekday sessions as reference categories.
- Excluded `PageValues` from the initial models because of potential outcome leakage.
- Retained product page count despite its strong correlation with browsing duration, pending multicollinearity assessment.

## Model Comparison

| Metric | Model 1 | Model 2 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Parameters | 5 | 6 |
| Residual deviance | 10,100.94 | 10,080.25 |
| AIC | 10,110.94 | 10,092.25 |

A likelihood ratio test indicated that including the interaction term significantly improved model fit:

- Chi-square statistic: 20.688
- Degrees of freedom: 1
- P-value: 5.406e-06
- AIC reduction: 18.69

Model 2 was selected as the preliminary preferred model, subject to diagnostic assessment.

## Statistical Inference Results

The interaction between browsing duration and visitor type was negative and statistically significant, indicating that the positive association between browsing duration and purchase odds is weaker for returning visitors than for new visitors.

| Visitor Type | Odds Ratio per Minute | 95% CI | Change in Odds | P-value |
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|
| New visitors | 1.0255 | (1.0161, 1.0349) | +2.55% | <0.001 |
| Returning visitors | 1.0046 | (1.0016, 1.0077) | +0.46% | 0.0029 |

Holding product page count and weekend status constant:

- Each additional minute of product browsing is associated with approximately 2.55% higher purchase odds for new visitors.
- Each additional minute of product browsing is associated with approximately 0.46% higher purchase odds for returning visitors.
- Both visitor-specific associations are statistically significant.

Full coefficient tables, standard errors, confidence intervals, p-values, and interpretations are provided in Section B of the QMD.

## Predicted Purchase Probabilities

Predicted purchase probability curves were generated using Model 2 with pointwise 95% confidence intervals.

Prediction conditions:

- Product page count was held constant at its median value of 18 pages.
- Weekend status was held constant at weekday.
- Browsing duration was varied from zero to its 95th percentile.
- Predictions were generated separately for new and returning visitors.

For weekday sessions involving 18 product page visits:

| Browsing Duration | New Visitors | Returning Visitors |
|---|---:|---:|
| 20 minutes | Approximately 28% | Approximately 12% |
| 60 minutes | Approximately 52% | Approximately 14% |

The predicted probability curves illustrate a stronger positive association between browsing duration and purchase likelihood among new visitors.

These predictions are conditional on the selected covariate values and do not represent average purchase probabilities across all visitors.

Given the strong correlation between product page count and browsing duration, predictions at longer durations should be interpreted cautiously until the observed data support for these combinations has been assessed.

The findings describe statistical associations rather than causal relationships.

### Important Considerations

- Product browsing duration is strongly right-skewed.
- Product page count and browsing duration are strongly correlated (r = 0.861).
- Bounce rate and exit rate are strongly correlated (r = 0.913).
- The overall purchase conversion rate is 15.45%, indicating outcome imbalance.
- The longest browsing duration group contains relatively few new visitors.
- New visitors are the visitor-type reference category.
- Weekday sessions are the weekend reference category.
- Identical records were retained provisionally because unique session identifiers are unavailable.
- Statistical significance and lower AIC do not establish that all model assumptions are satisfied.
- A skewed predictor does not, by itself, violate logistic regression assumptions.
